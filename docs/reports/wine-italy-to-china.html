4. PESTEL Analysis

  • Political: One-party state with active industrial policy (Made in China 2025, dual circulation). Geopolitical tension with the EU and US; retaliation risk in trade disputes (e.g. EU brandy, pork, dairy probes).
  • Economic: World's second-largest economy and a huge consumer market, but slower growth, weak consumer confidence and strong price competition from domestic champions. Latest: GDP growth 5.0% (2025), inflation 0.1% (World Bank).
  • Social: Digitally native consumers; 'guochao' preference for domestic brands among younger buyers; foreign brands win on safety, heritage and premium positioning.
  • Technological: World-leading e-commerce and super-apps (Tmall, JD, Douyin, WeChat); fast product cycles; strong domestic manufacturing capability.
  • Environmental: Dual-carbon goals (peak 2030, neutrality 2060) drive demand for clean-tech; growing ESG disclosure requirements.
  • Legal: Foreign Investment Law (2020) improved access; still complex licensing, local standards (GB/CCC), data-localisation rules and uneven IP enforcement. Sanitary and phytosanitary rules can block market access entirely.

6. Tariffs, Trade Defence & Regulation

⚠️ Trade defence: EU wine faces strong competition from Australia in China since China lifted its duties on Australian wine in 2024.

  • GACC registration of overseas food manufacturers (Decree 248) and Chinese labelling (Decree 249)
  • Market-access protocols needed for many meat, dairy and fresh products
  • Customs registration of the consignee; Chinese-language labelling for retail goods
  • Foreign Investment Law (2020) and the Negative List: most sectors open to wholly foreign-owned enterprises (WFOE)
  • Data Security Law and Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) for any customer data collected
  • Cross-border e-commerce (e.g. Tmall Global, JD Worldwide) allows testing demand under a lighter regime for many consumer goods

Verify current duties and requirements: - China Customs tariff and regulations (GACC) - MOFCOM trade remedy notices - EU Access2Markets: China export requirements - EU SME Centre (China market guides)

8. Recommended Entry Mode

Exclusive Chinese importer-distributor, supported by cross-border e-commerce

  • Food and beverage imports need a licensed Chinese importer for customs, GACC compliance and labelling.
  • Cross-border e-commerce builds brand awareness with consumers before scaling through general trade.

10. 90-Day Action Plan

  1. Days 1-30, validate: confirm the HS code and duty rate on official tariff tools, size the niche with customer interviews, shortlist 10 potential partners or channels in China.
  2. Days 1-30, compliance: map required certifications and labelling (see section 6), get quotes from a test lab or regulatory agent, register trademarks in the target market.
  3. Days 1-30, registration: file the product registration / notification early: it is the critical path.
  4. Days 31-60, commercial model: build a landed-cost and pricing model (duties, freight, VAT, channel margins, FX), and draft the partner or channel agreement.
  5. Days 31-60, go-to-market: localise product pages, packaging and sales material; pick 1-2 launch channels consistent with the entry mode: exclusive chinese importer-distributor, supported by cross-border e-commerce.
  6. Days 61-90, pilot: ship a pilot order or open the online store, track sell-through, margin and customer feedback against go/no-go KPIs.
